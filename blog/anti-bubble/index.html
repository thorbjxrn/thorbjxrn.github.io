The anti-bubble: market dysphoria

By Thorbjørn Bonvik · 13 November 2026

I think we’re in the anti-bubble. Market dysphoria.

You know the old saying: when your taxi driver and your grandmother start giving you stock tips, you’re near the top. Well, we have almost the opposite right now. People are fearful, angry, full of ressentiment (in the Nietzschean sense). There’s a bitterness to it, partly because they feel they’ve missed out on the crazy returns we’ve seen in tech. And every drawdown brings out the same warnings that we’re about to relive 2000.

But isn’t that kind of the point? Everyone is looking for the top. That doesn’t strike me as peak behaviour. People own the future, but they don’t seem particularly happy about owning it.

Meanwhile, I don’t think this thing can be stopped. Earnings continue to grow, and AI products are only beginning to find their mass-adoptable forms. I suspect physical AI, robotics and automation will be one of those moments where people suddenly go: “Ah, okay. I get it now.” But there will be many such moments. For me it was Claude 4.6, and some truly magnificent pieces of AI-generated art.

I feel a bit early right now. I think I can see where this is going, but the old guard isn’t convinced yet. I suspect they will be.

And I suspect the eventual bull market will be tech-led but ubiquitous. Maybe all this boring rotation is just laying the groundwork for the next leg higher. Or maybe the rotation game keeps chugging along without much progress for a while.

But when the psychology finally flips, when the fear and bitterness turn into belief and eventually FOMO, that’s when I think the real bull market turns into a bubble.

I give it about two years, admittedly borrowing a bit lazily from the 2000 timeline. But then again, the whole point is that every time is different.

The anti-bubble becomes the bubble.
